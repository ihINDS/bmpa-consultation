        The three tiers

        POM

        Prescription Only Medicine. Supplied by a pharmacist on a practitioner's prescription (Act s.34). Any new class or molecular structure is POM until reclassified.

        P

        Pharmacy Only. No prescription, but sold by the pharmacist from an area the public cannot reach, with advice or referral as needed.

        GSM

        General Sale Medicine. Sold outside a pharmacy only when the product, ingredient, strength, form and pack size are designated by the Authority. General sale products need a patient information leaflet (MPR 48).

        Chronic-disease bridge supply: up to 10 days for diabetes, hypertension, asthma, epilepsy or other chronic disease, where a prescription not older than six months is lodged.

        Where Barbados fits

        WHO maturity levels, PAHO, and the regulators the BMPA can rely on

        WHO measures regulators with its Global Benchmarking Tool (GBT), on a scale of four maturity levels. PAHO supports Barbados through this process. The Act lets the BMPA rely on trusted regulators' decisions, taking WHO's list of trusted authorities into account (Act s.57).

          What maturity levels are used for

          • A standard global measure of how a regulator performs.
          • Identifying gaps and strengths in a national regulatory authority.
          • Guiding the country's Institutional Development Plan.
          • Supporting WHO programmes such as WHO Listed Authority designation, which builds on sustained high performance.

          WHO's GBT and manuals are available in several official WHO languages: who.int/tools/global-benchmarking-tools

              About the Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (CPP)

              A CPP is issued by the regulator of the exporting country under the WHO Certification Scheme. It confirms the product is authorised there and made under GMP. The draft relies on it for the accelerated route (MPR 19, 22).

              • The certifying country doesn't have to be where the finished product is made or exported from, provided GMP is declared.
              • Recipient regulators shouldn't normally require CPPs from more than one authority.
              • A separate GMP certificate isn't needed when the CPP includes a GMP declaration; inspection is a risk-based decision.
              • Legalisation of a CPP isn't part of the Scheme and adds no assurance.
              • Alternatives include approval letters from established regulators, EMA opinions, European Commission decisions and regulator website listings.

              Source: WHO Certification Scheme Q&A, WHO Drug Information Vol. 30 No. 3 (2016).
